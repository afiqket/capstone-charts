This chart is a network/bubble visualization of Premier League clubs and their managers. Each **club is drawn as a bubble whose size is scaled to its "Number of managers"** value — in the code, `radius` for a Club is `8 + Math.sqrt(d.value) * 5`, where `d.value` is the manager count from `points.csv`.

So a **large club bubble suggests the club has cycled through many managers** over its history — i.e. low managerial stability, frequent hiring and firing, and likely turbulence or inconsistent results.

You can see this directly in the data. The biggest bubbles belong to clubs with the highest manager counts:

- **Chelsea** and **Tottenham Hotspur** — 57 each
- **Southampton** — 49
- **Newcastle United** — 48
- **Aston Villa** and **Crystal Palace** — 44 each
- **Everton** — 40
- **West Bromwich Albion** — 36

By contrast, the smallest bubbles are clubs with stable or short top‑flight histories — e.g. **Manchester United** (20, reflecting the long Ferguson era), **Arsenal** (18, long Wenger tenure), or clubs with brief spells like **Brentford**, **Blackpool**, **Luton Town**, **Swindon Town** (2 each).

In short: **a big bubble = many managers = a history of managerial churn / instability**, while a small bubble points to either managerial stability (long-serving managers) or simply a shorter overall history at the level being tracked.
