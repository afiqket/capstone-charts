You identify it through the **`Economy` column** in the data, which is encoded in the chart by **bar color**.

**In the data**, every row has an `Economy` value that is one of three categories:

| Region/Country | Economy |
|---|---|
| United States | U.S. |
| Canada | Developed |
| UK | Developed |
| Europe | Developed |
| China | Emerging |
| Japan | Developed |
| Emerging other | Emerging |
| Brazil | Emerging |

**In the chart**, the code maps each `Economy` category to a color, and fills each bar with `colors[d.economy]`:

- **U.S.** → pink `#ef4b92`
- **Developed** → teal `#2c8794`
- **Emerging** → grey `#9aa7a8`

So visually:
- **Developed**: Canada, UK, Europe, Japan (teal bars)
- **Emerging**: China, Emerging other, Brazil (grey bars)
- The U.S. is broken out separately (pink) rather than grouped with "Developed."

Two ways to read it off the chart itself:
1. **Match the bar color to the legend** at the top ("U.S." / "Developed" / "Emerging").
2. **Hover over any bar** — the tooltip shows an "Economy" row stating that country's category directly.

One note: China's bar also gets a distinct black outline (`stroke: #111`) to single it out, but its grey fill still marks it as Emerging.
