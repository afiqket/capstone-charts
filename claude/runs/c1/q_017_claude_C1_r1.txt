# Pattern: Inland vs Coastal Percent Change

**Inland states trend positive; coastal states trend negative.**

## Inland states (avg ≈ +1.5%)
The largest *increases* in past-month alcohol use are concentrated inland, especially in the Mountain West and Plains:
- Utah **+17.5**, New Mexico **+13.1**, Nebraska **+10.5**, Kansas **+7.4**, Iowa **+6.2**, South Dakota **+5.3**, Arizona **+4.6**, West Virginia **+4.5**
- Most inland states show positive or near-zero changes; the few notable inland declines are Idaho (−9.0) and Colorado (−6.4).

## Coastal states (avg ≈ −2.5%)
Coastal states are dominated by *decreases*, on both seaboards and the Gulf:
- **Atlantic:** North Carolina −9.9, South Carolina −6.2, Massachusetts −6.1, New Jersey −5.7, Delaware −4.9, Virginia −4.8, Connecticut −3.7
- **Gulf:** Mississippi −8.6, Alabama −5.4, Texas −5.0
- **Pacific:** Hawaii −8.7, Washington/Oregon/Alaska all near −2
- Coastal exceptions are modest positives: Louisiana +6.6, Florida +4.9, Maine +4.2, New Hampshire +3.1.

## Summary
The map shows a roughly **interior-vs-edge gradient**: a band of strong blue (large positive change) running through the interior West and Plains, surrounded by tan/light-blue (declines or near-zero change) along nearly every coastline. The extreme positive outliers are all landlocked, while coastlines hold most of the deepest declines.
