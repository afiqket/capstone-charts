Looking at the deaths data across the wars in chronological order:

**Early wars (late 1700s–mid 1800s):** Moderate death tolls in the tens of thousands.
- American Revolutionary War: 25,000
- War of 1812: 15,000
- Mexican–American War: 13,283

**Mid-19th century — a massive spike:**
- American Civil War: 750,000 (by far the deadliest, since both sides were US military)

**Late 19th / early 20th century — back down, then rising sharply with industrial-scale war:**
- Philippine–American War: 4,196
- World War I: 116,516
- World War II: 405,399

**Cold War era — high but lower than the world wars:**
- Korean War: 54,246
- Vietnam War: 58,209

**Modern wars — dramatic drop:**
- Gulf War: 294
- Iraq/Afghanistan Wars: 6,717

**Overall trend:** Deaths rose from the tens of thousands in early wars, peaked catastrophically with the Civil War (750K) and again with WWII (~405K), then fell sharply in the modern era. Today's conflicts (Gulf, Iraq/Afghanistan) have death tolls orders of magnitude smaller than the 20th-century world wars — likely reflecting better medicine, body armor, precision weapons, and smaller deployed forces.
