In the **Bar chart view**, clicking on the slider snaps it to the nearest year and re-draws the bars to reflect that year.

Here's the mechanism:

**The click target** — There's a transparent `rect` overlaying the slider track (lines 357–365) with an `on("click", …)` handler. (The handle circle itself only responds to *dragging*, line 367; plain clicks are caught by this overlay rect.)

**On click:**
1. It reads the mouse x-position (`d3.pointer`) and finds the year whose position on the slider scale (`xSlider`) is closest to the click (lines 362–364).
2. It calls `updateYear(y)` with that snapped year.

**Inside `updateYear` (lines 345–354):**
- Sets `currentYear = y` and moves the handle circle to that year's position (`handle.attr("cx", xSlider(y))`).
- Because `viewMode === "bar"`, it takes the `else` branch:
  - Updates the title to include the year: **`"Share of countries that have had a woman as leader, {y}"`** (line 351).
  - Calls **`drawBar()`** (line 352), which redraws the two horizontal bars ("Any woman chief executive" and "Democratically-elected woman chief executive") using that selected year's values, with bar widths and the `%` labels updated accordingly.

So clicking anywhere on the slider jumps to the nearest year and the bar chart updates to show that year's two percentages, with the year appended to the title.

(For contrast, in Line view the same click still moves the handle and sets `currentYear`, but it only resets the title to the plain text and does **not** otherwise change the line chart — the year selection has no visible effect there.)
