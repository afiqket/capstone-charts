Looking at the data — these are each component's contribution (in percentage points) to real GDP growth that quarter:

| Quarter | Real GDP | Investment | Net exports |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jul-22** | 2.7% | **−1.05** | **+2.50** |
| **Jan-25** | −0.2% | **+3.98** | **−4.90** |

**The two roles essentially flipped.**

- **Jul-22:** Net exports were the engine, adding +2.5 pts (the single largest positive contributor that quarter), while Investment was a drag, subtracting −1.05 pts. Despite the investment drag, the strong trade contribution helped keep GDP solidly positive at +2.7%.

- **Jan-25:** The opposite happened. Investment surged to +3.98 pts — a large positive boost — but Net exports collapsed to −4.9 pts, the biggest single drag in the entire dataset. The trade drag more than wiped out the investment gain (and everything else), pulling GDP slightly negative at −0.2%.

**In short:** In Jul-22, net exports lifted GDP while investment weighed it down. In Jan-25 the polarity reversed — investment lifted GDP while a massive net-exports drag dominated and tipped overall growth negative.
