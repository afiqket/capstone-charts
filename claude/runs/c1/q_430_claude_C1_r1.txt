## Answer: Coffee

Comparing the price index values for 1958 with the most recent year in the data (2020), coffee shows the largest change in magnitude.

The chart measures prices as an index relative to 1900 = 100. Taking each commodity's 1958 value (row 110) versus its 2020 value (row 172):

| Food | 1958 | 2020 | Change |
|------|------|------|--------|
| **Coffee** | **164.89** | **50.58** | **−114.3** |
| Beef | 114.70 | 196.38 | +81.7 |
| Tea | 90.47 | 26.59 | −63.9 |
| Corn | 92.08 | 28.77 | −63.3 |
| Peanuts | 96.24 | 37.57 | −58.7 |
| Wheat | 81.84 | 26.39 | −55.5 |
| Cocoa | 74.22 | 21.39 | −52.8 |
| Pork | 56.57 | 10.80 | −45.8 |
| Lamb | 134.29 | 165.62 | +31.3 |

**Coffee** changed the most — its price index fell by about **114 points** (a ~69% drop), the largest absolute move of any commodity.

A couple of caveats on interpretation:
- If you mean the largest **increase**, it's **Beef** (+81.7, from 114.7 to 196.4).
- If you mean the largest **percentage** change, it's **Pork**, which fell ~81% (56.57 → 10.80).

But by the most direct reading — the biggest overall swing in price — the answer is **coffee**.
